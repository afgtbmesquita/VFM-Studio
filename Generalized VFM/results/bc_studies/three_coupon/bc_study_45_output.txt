==============================================================================
  WHY D12 IS THE MOST SENSITIVE COEFFICIENT
==============================================================================
  Lab-frame basis matrices at 45 deg, NORMAL block only:
              [0,0]    [0,1]    [1,1]
       D11     0.25     0.25     0.25
       D12     0.50     0.50     0.50
       D22     0.25     0.25     0.25
       D66     1.00    -1.00     1.00

  D11, D12 and D22 all produce the SAME pattern (+1,+1,+1) x their
  coefficient / 4, with D12 counted twice.  So in the normal
  channels the 45 deg coupon sees one number only:

      S = (D11 + D22 + 2 D12) / 4        and, separately, D66

  It cannot separate D11, D12 and D22 at all -- that separation has
  to come from the two axis-aligned coupons.  Inverting for D12:

      D12 = 2 S - (D11 + D22) / 2

    2 S            =     96.64 N.mm
    (D11 + D22)/2  =    104.58 N.mm
    D12            =     -7.94 N.mm   <- the difference

  A DIFFERENCE OF LARGE NUMBERS: 105 - 105 ~ -8.  The cancellation is
  13-fold, so a 1 % error in EITHER term becomes a 13 % error in D12.

  Sensitivity of D12 to each input, all else held:
    dD12/dS   = 2      -> 1 N.mm of S   moves D12 by 2.0 N.mm
    dD12/dD11 = -1/2   -> 1 N.mm of D11 moves D12 by 0.5 N.mm
    dD12/dD22 = -1/2   -> 1 N.mm of D22 moves D12 by 0.5 N.mm

  And S is the 45 deg coupon's MAGNITUDE observable -- the one the
  moment gradient contaminates -- while its ratio observable
  (the anticlastic ratio) carries D66 instead.  So D12 inherits
  twice the error of the least trustworthy thing this coupon
  measures, on top of half the error of D11, which the campaign
  already could not pin down.  That is the whole explanation.

  IT IS NOT A 45 DEG PROBLEM PER SE.  D12 was already unresolved on
  two coupons (0.44 to 2.07 depending on BC form, CI +/- 4.3).  The
  45 deg coupon does not make D12 worse; it makes the reason
  VISIBLE, because it is the first test that constrains the
  combination D12 sits inside at all.
