==============================================================================
  THE D66 CROSS-CHECK -- closed form vs the full identification
==============================================================================
  At 45 deg with traction-free lateral edges the measured
  anticlastic ratio r = kappa_xx/kappa_yy fixes D66 in closed form:
      r = -(S - D66)/(S + D66)   with   S = (D11 + D22 + 2 D12)/4
      =>  D66 = S (1 + r) / (1 - r)
  This uses ONLY the two normal curvature channels of the 45 deg
  coupon and the D11, D22, D12 the other two coupons supply.  It is
  an independent route to the same number, and it is immune to the
  specimen width and the moment gradient, both of which cancel from
  a ratio of curvatures.

==============================================================================
  CUT SENSE: IS THE MATERIAL 1-AXIS AT +45 OR +135 DEG?
==============================================================================
  The coupon length runs along its own +y (90 deg from +x) and the
  material 1-axis is 45 deg from the length, so the 1-axis is at
  either 90-45 = 45 deg or 90+45 = 135 deg.  Both are geometrically
  possible; only the data can say which.

  T(45)^T B66 T(45)  diag/offdiag = +1.00 / -1.00
  T(135)^T B66 T(135) diag/offdiag = +1.00 / -1.00
    -> identical.  D66 cannot distinguish the two senses, by
       construction, so the ambiguity is harmless for it.

  phi = 45 deg  (shipped)               153.31     -7.94     55.84      9.87        2418
  phi = 135 deg (other sense)           124.95     -6.48     59.10     10.69        2355
  |difference|                           28.36      1.46      3.26      0.83

  The two senses differ by up to 18.5 %.  The cut sense
  MUST then be established independently (fixture photographs,
